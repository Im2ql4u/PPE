# Vision

## The idea in one sentence

**Every passage you are learning becomes a small laboratory.** You see it through every available interpretation, perturb it to discover what is essential, test your ear and hands before you're told, and see honestly what you actually did.

## Philosophy

Stop treating the piano as 88 independent keys; experience it as a landscape of relationships.
The research turned that into something testable:

> A relationship is what stays **invariant under transformation**.

A progression survives transposition. A harmonic skeleton survives its elaborations. A function survives re-voicing. A phrase shape survives a change of tempo.
You come to *know* a relationship by changing something and hearing what persists, the way a physicist learns a system by perturbing it.
So the environment is built around four verbs (`product-concepts.md` §3):

| Verb | Question it answers |
|---|---|
| **Look through lenses** | What do the analysts, the models, the great pianists and I each say this passage *is*, and where do we disagree? |
| **Transform** | What happens if this chord were different, this voice removed, this passage in another key, this rubato exaggerated? |
| **Probe** | Can I hear it, predict it, sing it, play it in another key, before being told? |
| **Mirror** | What did I actually do, over days and weeks, and is it changing? |

## Commitments

1. **Interpretation is plural and labelled.** Every claim about the music has an author: a named analyst, a model and version, a recording, or me. Where experts disagree (they do, measurably: E1, E2), the disagreement is shown, not resolved.
2. **Facts come from the score, not from the language model.** The tutor explains; it never invents analysis.
3. **Hear and do before being told.** Prediction precedes revelation. Every lens leads to the keyboard.
4. **Learning is measured cold.** The first unassisted attempt next session counts, not the twentieth today.
5. **Expression is not error.** Rubato, voicing and pedal are musical quantities to understand, never scores to maximise.
6. **No gamification.** No streaks, badges or percentages. The reward is hearing more.
7. **Local and mine.** Recordings and learning history stay on my machine, inspectable and queryable.

## The three fluencies, and how each is served

| Fluency | Primary verbs | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Physical: moving naturally around the keyboard | Probe, Mirror | Transposition probes; keyboard-shape views per function; restart maps; consistency maps; n-of-1 practice experiments |
| Harmonic: hearing and recognising structure | Lens, Transform, Probe | Two-analyst views; reduction ladder; counterfactual chords; minimal-pair listening; repertoire-derived ear training |
| Musical: imagining and expressing | Transform, Probe, Mirror | Audiation probes; improvising onto skeletons; hearing your own shaping exaggerated or neutralised; reference pianists' choices |

## Who it is for

One adult learner: an early-stage pianist with a physicist's mind. Their reference repertoire runs from Bach's C-major prelude to Rachmaninoff's second concerto (`repertoire.md`).
The design must be useful at today's level and must not cap tomorrow's. Data regimes range from richly annotated (Moonlight, Clair de lune, BWV 846) to private arrangements with nothing but a score.

## What a session might feel like (illustrative)

You open Moonlight at bar 15. The score shows a few notes in orange: the two analysts disagree.
Before reading anything you press *hear*, and the app asks: does bar 16 sound like it belongs to B minor or E minor?
You choose, then both readings appear: Chen & Su's Neapolitan in B minor, Tymoczko's VI in E minor (with his note: "pretty hard to identify").
You strip the triplets away (reduction ladder) and hear the bare chords. You swap the chord for an ordinary iv and hear what Beethoven didn't write.
Then you play bars 15–18 in D minor and discover your left hand only knew the C♯-minor finger pattern.
Tomorrow the app asks you to play bars 15–18 cold. The mirror shows you stopped at bar 16 four times last week.
